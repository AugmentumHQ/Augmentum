## Classic Beef Bourguignon

**Serves:** 6 | **Active time:** 45 minutes | **Total time:** 3 hours 45 minutes | **Difficulty:** Intermediate

Few dishes reward patience like a proper bourguignon. The wine braise transforms tough chuck into something silky, and the lardons and pearl onions give it depth that no shortcut version can replicate. Do not use "cooking wine" — it must be something you'd drink.

### Ingredients

**For the braise:**
- 3 lbs beef chuck, cut into 2-inch cubes, patted completely dry
- 8 oz thick-cut bacon or lardons, cut into ½-inch pieces
- 1 bottle (750ml) Burgundy or other full-bodied Pinot Noir
- 2 cups beef stock (low-sodium, homemade preferred)
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1 bouquet garni (thyme, bay leaves, flat-leaf parsley)
- 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 2 tablespoons neutral oil (grapeseed or canola)
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper

**For the garnish:**
- 1 lb cremini mushrooms, quartered
- 1 lb frozen pearl onions, thawed (or fresh, blanched and peeled)
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 teaspoon sugar

### Method

1. **Marinate (optional but recommended):** The night before, combine beef, wine, garlic, and bouquet garni. Refrigerate overnight. Drain, reserving marinade. Pat beef completely dry with paper towels — this step is non-negotiable for proper browning.

2. **Render the lardons:** In a large Dutch oven over medium heat, cook lardons until fat is rendered and edges are crispy, about 8 minutes. Remove with a slotted spoon; leave fat in pot.

3. **Sear the beef:** Working in batches of no more than 6 pieces (do not crowd the pan), sear beef in the lardon fat over high heat until deeply browned on all sides, 3–4 minutes per side. Season with salt and pepper after each batch. Remove to a plate.

4. **Deglaze:** Pour off all but 2 tablespoons of fat. Add tomato paste and cook, stirring, for 2 minutes. Deglaze with reserved marinade or fresh wine, scraping up all browned bits from the bottom. Add stock.

5. **Braise:** Return beef and lardons to the pot. Liquid should come about three-quarters up the meat. Bring to a simmer, then transfer to a **325°F (163°C)** oven, uncovered. Braise for **3 hours**, checking every 45 minutes and adding stock if needed. Beef is done when a fork slides in with zero resistance.

6. **Prepare the garnish:** In a skillet, melt butter over medium-high heat. Add pearl onions, sugar, and a pinch of salt. Cook until glazed and golden, about 12 minutes. Remove. In the same pan, sauté mushrooms in butter until browned, about 7 minutes. Set aside.

7. **Finish:** Remove beef. Strain braising liquid into a saucepan, pressing on solids. Simmer over medium-high heat until reduced by one-third and glossy, about 15 minutes. Adjust seasoning. Return beef, onions, and mushrooms to the pot.

**Serve over:** Buttered egg noodles, crusty bread, or mashed potatoes.

## Thai Green Curry

**Serves:** 4 | **Total time:** 20 minutes | **Difficulty:** Easy

The secret to a bright green curry is not overcooking the paste — you want to "bloom" it briefly in the coconut cream layer that rises to the top of the can, then add aromatics. Speed is the virtue here; this dish should come together faster than rice takes to cook.

### Ingredients

- 1.5 lbs boneless chicken thighs, sliced ¼-inch thick (or firm tofu for vegetarian)
- 3–4 tablespoons Mae Ploy or Maesri green curry paste
- 2 cans (13.5 oz each) full-fat coconut milk — do not shake
- 1 cup chicken stock
- 2 tablespoons fish sauce (or soy sauce)
- 1 tablespoon palm sugar (or light brown sugar)
- 4 kaffir lime leaves, central rib removed, chiffonade
- 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and cut into 2-inch pieces
- 1-inch knob galangal, sliced thin (substitute ginger only if unavailable)
- 1 cup Thai eggplant, quartered (or regular eggplant, cubed)
- 1 cup fresh baby spinach or Thai basil leaves
- 1 red chili, sliced thin, for garnish
- Steamed jasmine rice, to serve

### Method

1. **Separate the coconut cream:** Open the cans without shaking. Scoop the thick cream from the top (about ½ cup per can) into a large wok or sauté pan. Reserve the thin coconut milk below.

2. **Bloom the paste:** Heat the coconut cream over medium-high heat. Add curry paste and cook, stirring constantly, until fragrant and paste has "fried" in the fat, about 3–4 minutes. The cream will separate and look oily — this is correct.

3. **Build the aromatics:** Add lemongrass and galangal. Stir 1 minute.

4. **Add liquids:** Pour in remaining coconut milk, chicken stock, fish sauce, and palm sugar. Bring to a simmer. Add kaffir lime leaves.

5. **Cook the protein:** Add chicken and eggplant. Simmer over medium heat (do not boil hard) until chicken is cooked through, about 8–10 minutes. Taste and adjust fish sauce for salt, sugar for sweetness.

6. **Finish:** Remove from heat. Fold in fresh basil leaves. Garnish with sliced red chili. Serve immediately over jasmine rice — curry waits for no one.

## Sourdough Bread

**Makes:** 1 loaf (~950g) | **Active time:** 1 hour | **Total time:** 24 hours (including fermentation) | **Difficulty:** Advanced

Sourdough is less a recipe than a practice. Variables like ambient temperature, starter activity, and flour protein content all affect the outcome. These instructions assume a mature, active starter that doubles within 4–6 hours of feeding. A room temperature of 75–78°F (24–26°C) is ideal.

### Ingredients

- 450g bread flour (12–13% protein; King Arthur Bread Flour recommended)
- 50g whole wheat flour
- 375g filtered water (divided: 325g + 50g)
- 100g active sourdough starter (100% hydration, fed 4–8 hours prior)
- 10g fine sea salt

### Method

**Autolyse (12:00 PM):**
Combine 450g bread flour, 50g whole wheat flour, and 325g water (room temperature) in a large bowl. Mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 45–60 minutes. The autolyse hydrates the flour and begins gluten development without mechanical effort.

**Add starter (1:00 PM):**
Dimple the autolysed dough and add starter. Squeeze through fingers to incorporate. Once combined, add salt dissolved in the remaining 50g water. Continue mixing by folding and pressing for 4–5 minutes until dough feels cohesive.

**Bulk fermentation (1:15 PM – 5:15 PM, approximately 4 hours):**
During the first 2 hours, perform 4 sets of stretch-and-fold every 30 minutes: wet one hand, grab the north edge of the dough, stretch high, fold to center; rotate the bowl 90° and repeat four times per set. After 4 sets, leave undisturbed. Dough is ready to shape when it has increased 50–75% in volume, bubbles appear on the surface and sides, and the mass jiggles like gelatin when the bowl is shaken.

**Shape and cold proof (5:30 PM – next day):**
Turn dough onto an unfloured surface. Pre-shape into a round; bench rest 20 minutes. Final-shape into a tight batard or boule using the letter-fold method, generating surface tension. Place seam-side up in a floured banneton (rice flour prevents sticking). Cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate 12–16 hours.

**Bake (next morning):**
1. Place a Dutch oven (5.5 qt or larger) in the oven. Preheat to **500°F (260°C)** for at least 45 minutes.
2. Invert cold dough onto parchment. Score with a lame or sharp razor at a 30° angle, ½-inch deep.
3. Carefully lower dough into the screaming-hot Dutch oven using the parchment as a sling.
4. Bake covered at **500°F for 20 minutes** (steam phase). Remove lid; reduce to **450°F (232°C)** and bake an additional **20–25 minutes** until deeply browned (internal temp 205–210°F / 96–99°C).
5. Cool on a wire rack **at least 1 hour** before cutting. Cutting early releases steam and turns the crumb gummy.

## Chocolate Lava Cake

**Serves:** 4 | **Active time:** 20 minutes | **Bake time:** 12 minutes at 425°F | **Difficulty:** Easy

The "lava" center is not raw batter — it is an intentionally underbaked ganache center achieved by precise timing. Over-bake by even two minutes and you get a dense brownie. Under-bake and the exterior hasn't set. Use an oven thermometer; most home ovens run 25°F off.

### Ingredients

- 6 oz (170g) 70% dark chocolate, finely chopped (Valrhona Guanaja or Scharffen Berger)
- 6 tablespoons (85g) unsalted butter, cut into pieces, plus more for ramekins
- 2 large eggs
- 2 large egg yolks
- ¼ cup (50g) granulated sugar
- 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
- Pinch of fleur de sel
- 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- Cocoa powder, for dusting ramekins
- Vanilla ice cream or crème fraîche, to serve

### Method

1. **Prepare ramekins:** Butter four 6-ounce ramekins thoroughly, then dust with cocoa powder, tapping out excess. This prevents sticking and adds a subtle bitter crust. Refrigerate until needed.

2. **Melt chocolate:** Combine chocolate and butter in a heatproof bowl set over a saucepan of barely simmering water (bain-marie). Stir occasionally until completely smooth. Remove from heat; cool 5 minutes.

3. **Make the batter:** Whisk together eggs, egg yolks, sugar, vanilla, and salt in a bowl until pale yellow and slightly thickened, about 2 minutes. Gradually fold the warm chocolate mixture into the egg mixture (do not whisk — fold to preserve volume). Sift flour over the top; fold until just incorporated.

4. **Fill and refrigerate:** Divide batter evenly among prepared ramekins. At this point, cakes can be refrigerated up to 24 hours. (Add 1 minute to baking time if baking from cold.)

5. **Bake:** Preheat oven to **425°F (218°C)**. Bake ramekins on a baking sheet for exactly **12 minutes** — edges should be set and pulling away slightly from the sides, center should have a visible wobble. Do not open the oven before 11 minutes.

6. **Unmold:** Let rest 1 minute. Run a knife around the inside edge. Place a dessert plate on top and invert in one confident motion. Lift ramekin. Serve immediately with ice cream.

## Mediterranean Quinoa Salad

**Serves:** 6 | **Total time:** 30 minutes (no cooking required for assembly) | **Difficulty:** Easy

This is a no-cook assembly once the quinoa is prepared. The overnight rest dramatically improves the flavors as the grains absorb the lemon-herb dressing. It travels well, making it ideal for packed lunches and potlucks. Use tri-color quinoa for visual appeal.

### Ingredients

**Salad base:**
- 1½ cups dry quinoa, rinsed thoroughly
- 2¼ cups water or vegetable broth
- 1 English cucumber, diced small (no need to peel)
- 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
- ½ red onion, very finely diced
- 1 cup Kalamata olives, pitted and halved
- 1 cup canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 4 oz feta cheese, crumbled (not pre-crumbled — block feta has better flavor)
- ½ cup roasted red peppers, drained and sliced
- ¼ cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, roughly chopped
- 2 tablespoons fresh mint, chiffonade

**Lemon-herb dressing:**
- 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice (about 1 lemon)
- 1 teaspoon lemon zest
- 1 small garlic clove, minced to a paste with salt
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- ½ teaspoon ground cumin
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

### Method

1. **Cook quinoa:** Bring quinoa and water (or broth) to a boil. Reduce to low, cover, and simmer 15 minutes until liquid is absorbed. Remove from heat; fluff with a fork. Spread on a sheet pan to cool quickly to room temperature.

2. **Make dressing:** Whisk all dressing ingredients together. Taste on a piece of bread — it should be bright and assertive (it will mellow once it coats the salad).

3. **Assemble:** In a large bowl, combine cooled quinoa, cucumber, tomatoes, red onion, olives, chickpeas, and roasted peppers. Drizzle with dressing; toss to coat. Fold in parsley, mint, and feta last to preserve texture.

4. **Rest:** Cover and refrigerate at least 30 minutes, preferably overnight. Re-season before serving.

## Miso Glazed Salmon

**Serves:** 4 | **Prep time:** 10 minutes + 30-minute marinate | **Cook time:** 8 minutes under broiler | **Difficulty:** Easy

The Nobu-style miso marinade is built on the principle of miso's amino acids caramelizing under high heat to create a lacquered crust that is simultaneously sweet, savory, and umami-forward. Use white (shiro) miso — red miso is too sharp and will burn before the fish cooks through.

### Ingredients

- 4 salmon fillets (6 oz each, skin-on, 1–1¼ inch thick; sockeye or king preferred)
- 3 tablespoons white (shiro) miso paste
- 2 tablespoons mirin
- 1 tablespoon sake (or dry sherry)
- 1 tablespoon granulated sugar
- 1 teaspoon soy sauce
- 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
- Thinly sliced scallions, toasted sesame seeds, and pickled ginger, to garnish
- Steamed short-grain rice and blanched baby bok choy, to serve

### Method

1. **Make marinade:** Whisk together miso, mirin, sake, sugar, soy sauce, and sesame oil until sugar dissolves and mixture is smooth. If miso is lumpy, warm briefly in a microwave to make it pliable.

2. **Marinate:** Pat salmon dry. Coat flesh side (not skin) with marinade in a thin, even layer. Place in a shallow dish, cover, and refrigerate at least 30 minutes and up to 8 hours. Do not marinate longer — the salt in miso will begin to cure and dry out the fish.

3. **Set up broiler:** Position oven rack 6 inches from broiler element. Preheat broiler to high (500°F / 260°C equivalent). Line a sheet pan with foil; coat with cooking spray or oil. The foil is essential — miso glaze will carbonize directly on the pan.

4. **Broil:** Place salmon skin-side down on prepared pan. Broil **8 minutes** without flipping. The glaze should be deeply caramelized and spotted black in places — this is intentional. The fish is done when it flakes at the thickest part with light pressure. Internal temperature should read 125–130°F (52–54°C) for medium.

5. **Serve:** Transfer carefully with a wide spatula (skin tends to stick to foil — that's fine, leave it). Garnish with scallions, sesame seeds, and pickled ginger. Serve immediately over rice.

*All oven temperatures assume a conventional (non-convection) oven. For convection, reduce temperature by 25°F and check doneness early.*
