# Support Augmentum's Development

## Need help instead?

If you came here from an error message or a broken install: [open an issue](https://github.com/AugmentumHQ/Augmentum/issues) for bugs, or [start a discussion](https://github.com/AugmentumHQ/Augmentum/discussions) for questions and setup help. Include your Augmentum version, OS, and the compose files you're running — it usually halves the round-trips. For security problems, see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) — please don't open public issues for those.

The rest of this page is about supporting the project itself.

---

Augmentum is free, and it's going to stay that way. The server, the modes, the substrate, the apps, the companion when it ships — every layer. If you want to help me keep it that way, this page is for you.

## Why I built it

Personal AI is being decided right now, mostly by companies whose pitch is: hand us your conversations, your memories, your work, and you'll get an assistant back. Your stuff lives in their cloud. The convenience is real and so is the price — you become legible to a system whose interests aren't yours.

I wanted the other shape. Local-first, on hardware you own, with memory that doesn't leak, federated with your friends instead of a vendor. The same modes you'd expect from a paid product — chat, narrative, analytical, agentic, coder — but yours. The hard engineering that makes small local models actually work (substrate, layered memory, cross-modal context) is what this page funds.

Somebody had to build the off-ramp from the AI silos. I want this to be it.

## You've probably already helped

If you installed Augmentum, that helped. If you filed a bug, suggested a feature, contributed code or docs, packaged a knowledge corpus, shared a character, or ran a fabric peer for a friend or family member — that helped more than any single sponsorship.

Honestly, the most common form of support is using the product and telling someone else about it. That's not consolation framing. It's the actual way this grows, and it's the way other developers learn that local-first AI is worth building. If that's all you ever do, you've done a lot.

## If you want to help further

**Use it as your daily AI.** It gets sharper when people live in it. One bug from a daily user is worth ten from a curious tire-kicker.

**Share it with someone it might help.** Someone who's been priced out of cloud AI, someone with privacy concerns, someone with accessibility needs the big providers don't serve, someone burned by a service shutdown. Word-of-mouth is the only growth channel that actually matches what the product is.

**Run a fabric peer.** Hosting Augmentum for friends or family extends local-first to people who can't run it themselves. That's federation working the way it's supposed to.

**Contribute back.** Code, docs, knowledge packs, translations, character cards, voice samples — anything the project ships. Reproducible bug reports count too.

**Sponsor development.**
- [GitHub Sponsors](https://github.com/sponsors/AugmentumHQ) — one-time or recurring, any amount
- [One-time tip](https://donate.stripe.com/dRm14pdwxcj5glcdQS0RG02) — pay what you want, no account needed
- Open Collective — coming once the project qualifies for fiscal hosting; the ledger will be public when it does

No supporter-only features. No badges. No tiered perks. What you get is the same whether you give nothing, five dollars, or five hundred. That's the deal and I'm not going to change it.

## Three things I won't do

These aren't aspirations. If any of them change, I've lost the plot and you should stop supporting me.

- **I won't sell you out.** No ads in the product or the docs. No telemetry without your explicit consent. No selling user data — there isn't user data on a server I control to sell, and that's on purpose.
- **I won't take features away.** If something works today, it'll work tomorrow on the same terms. No paywalls bolted onto things that used to be free. No "premium tier" carved out of features you already had.
- **I won't sell out to investors.** I'm not taking funding that comes with strings on the two promises above. If Augmentum grows beyond what I can sustain alone, it grows through community or stays small. Big-VC money is how local-first projects quietly turn into the thing they were built to replace.

## Where the money goes

Honest answer: most of it is bought time.

App store fees come first — Google Play is $25 once, Apple is $99 a year. Then code signing, the GitHub bill (CI minutes and release storage), the domain, hosting for the project site and docs. Hardware I buy for testing across actual deployment shapes — different GPUs, ARM SBCs, phones, accessibility devices. The kind of testing you can't fake.

Anything beyond that is hours. Hours go into the substrate — the boring infrastructure that makes local AI actually viable for people who shouldn't have to be sysadmins. Not into chasing growth metrics, because there's nothing to grow toward; the destination already exists.

The first ~$200 a year covers the app stores. The next ~$300 covers everything operational. After that, it's me, sitting down, building.
